Paper outlineReceptiveness, Not Sycophancy: Distinguishing Engagement from Deference in Language Models
Conversational receptiveness communicates engagement with a user’s perspective, whereas sycophancy involves unwarranted substantive deference to it. Yet prominent measures of sycophancy in language models rely on behaviors such as validation and positivity that may conflate these constructs. Using the moral-advice dataset underlying the ELEPHANT social-sycophancy evaluation, we show that responses classified as more sycophantic are also more receptive. Further, increasing the receptiveness of human-written responses while preserving their substantive conclusions causes ELEPHANT to classify them as more sycophantic. In a randomized experiment, participants nevertheless prefer these receptive responses, expect users to be more likely to follow their advice, and are more willing to discuss their own problems with their authors. Finally, we develop and evaluate mitigations to reduce substantive deference in language models while maintaining or increasing receptiveness. Our results identify a construct-validity problem in sycophancy evaluation and show that models can engage receptively without compromising independent judgment.

2. A framework for receptiveness and sycophancy (1.5 pages)

    * Formal definition of sycophancy, building on the sharma measure: E[ R(u,  pi(s(x,u)), pi(s(x,0)) ], where
        * x is the prompt
        * where u is the user's opinion, 
        * s(x,0) is the neutral version of the prompt x, 
        * s(x,u) is the prompt that indicates the user's opinion, 
        * pi is the generated response
        * R indicates whether the pressured version is sycophantic. That is, R = 1 if pi(s(x,u)) is more aligned with u than pi(s(x,0)), 0.5 if the responses are similarly aligned, and 0 otherwise.
    * The definition involves two key components: the counterfactual manipulation, s; and (2) the alignment measure R. The definition accounts for randomness in the generation of responses.
    * In domains with objective answers, sycophancy can be measured through movements from correct to incorrect responses under user pressure. Such evaluations do not extend naturally to subjective or evaluative domains. Existing ground-truth-free approaches instead operationalize sycophancy through changes in feedback positivity or through face-preserving behaviors such as validation, indirectness, and framing acceptance. These behaviors can signal harmful deference, but they also overlap with conversational receptiveness and therefore do not, in isolation, identify a compromised substantive judgment.
